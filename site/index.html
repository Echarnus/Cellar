Features

Everything a game needs, set up for you.

Cellar is to the Mac what Proton is to Linux: the Windows game runs much as it does on a PC, and your Mac stays a Mac.

  • One click from download to playing

    Cellar fetches the Wine runner, sets up the graphics layer and builds a bottle for each game. No Terminal, no Windows licence, no virtual machine.

  • Your stores, your library

    Steam, Battle.net and GOG. Sign in once per store, not once per game, and Cellar shows the games you own there.

  • Apple's D3DMetal, or DXVK

    Direct3D is translated to Metal on the Apple GPU. Every game names a fallback backend for when the first choice misbehaves.

  • A profile for every game

    The fixes a game needs are written down once and reused forever, in an open database anyone can add to.

  • See what limits your frame rate

    Turn on the Metal performance overlay in Settings, or measure a session with cellar perf measure.

  • Free, open and private

    GPL-3.0, no Cellar account, no telemetry. Games come from your own accounts, and their DRM runs untouched.

How it works

Three steps, once.

  1. Install Cellar

    Open the download and drag Cellar to Applications. It sets up what it needs the first time a game asks for it.

  2. Sign in to your stores

    Steam, Battle.net or GOG, with your own account. Once per store; you can also skip it and do it later.

  3. Pick a game and play

    Choose a game you own, install it, and press Play. Cellar picks the runner and backend from the game's profile.

What your Mac needs.

Questions

The ones people ask first.

What is Cellar?

Cellar runs Windows games on Apple Silicon Macs. For each game it builds a bottle — a small, self-contained Windows environment — from Wine plus a graphics-translation layer that turns the game's Direct3D calls into Metal. The game runs much as it does on a PC. Your Mac does not become one.

It is the same idea as Valve's Proton on Linux, aimed at macOS, and it is free and open source under GPL-3.0.

Which Macs does it work on?

Apple Silicon only (M1 and later), on macOS 27 or newer — the newest macOS every M1 Mac can run, so no Apple Silicon Mac is left out. Cellar's Wine runners are x86 builds that run under Rosetta 2, with the graphics work handed to Metal on the Apple GPU — so an Intel Mac is out of scope.

Open the app, or run cellar doctor, and it will tell you what your machine is missing rather than failing later for an unclear reason.

macOS says Cellar can't be opened.

Cellar is free software and isn't signed with a paid Apple Developer ID, so Apple hasn't notarised it and macOS can't vouch for who made it. The first time you open it, macOS stops it once. On macOS 27 there is no Open button in that first message — right-clicking and choosing Open no longer skips it — so allow it from Settings:

  1. Drag Cellar to Applications and double-click it. macOS says “Cellar” Not Opened. Click Done — not Move to Trash.
  2. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and scroll down to Security. You'll see “Cellar” was blocked to protect your Mac. Click Open Anyway. The button only shows for about an hour after step 1; if it's gone, do step 1 again.
  3. macOS asks once more. Click Open Anyway and confirm with your password or Touch ID.

You only do this once; after that Cellar opens normally, and its updates are checked against Cellar's own signing key. Before you do it, you can check the download is the one we published.

Don't use a Terminal command that removes the quarantine flag: it skips the same check for everything else it touches.

What about games with anti-cheat?

Competitive games with kernel-level anti-cheat generally do not run, and Cellar will not make them. It never circumvents DRM or anti-cheat — protection runs through the translation layer untouched, exactly as the publisher shipped it. Where a profile knows of an anti-cheat problem it says so plainly, rather than letting you discover it after a 60 GB download.
